
Sahaya (also operating under the assistant name "Shakthi") is a voice-first, zero-tech-barrier application designed to empower women and provide instant access to crucial safety, emergency, and welfare resources. Built specifically for users with limited digital literacy or those in high-stress panic situations, the app entirely eliminates complex navigation menus and typing. Instead, it relies on intuitive multilingual voice commands and massive, color-coded action buttons.

**Core Safety Measures & Features:**

* **Multilingual Voice Assistant:** The app features a tap-and-speak interface that understands and responds in multiple regional languages (including Hindi, English, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Marathi). Women can simply speak their problems or ask for help without needing to read or type.
* **One-Tap Emergency SOS (Red Zone):** A dedicated, highly visible emergency section allows users to instantly dial the Police (112) or the Women's Helpline (1091). The buttons are oversized and designed to be easily tapped even with shaking hands.
* **Trusted Contact Linking (Green Zone):** Users can link a husband, brother, or trusted guardian's phone number directly to the app. Once saved, a single tap will immediately dial this relative during a crisis.
* **Instant Location & Routing (Blue Zone):** Integrated directly with Google Maps, the app provides one-tap navigation to the nearest police station or hospital based on the user's live GPS location, helping them find safe havens instantly.
* **Local Community Support:** The app bridges the gap between digital tools and physical safety by providing direct contact options for local on-ground helpers, such as ASHA and Anganwadi workers, who can provide immediate, in-person assistance.

**Design Philosophy:**
Sahaya is structured to feel like a native mobile application with a progressive, color-coded vertical interface. It uses deep psychological color cues—Yellow for voice interaction, Red for immediate police emergencies, Blue for navigation, and Green for trusted family calling—ensuring that a woman in distress can locate exactly what she needs within seconds, regardless of her educational or technical background.
